## Device and format

Choose your model under **Device**. To set the page size yourself, open **Advanced conversion options** and fill in **Custom width** and **Custom height**, in pixels. With the **Other** device, both are required. On the command line these settings are `--profile`, `--format`, `--customwidth` and `--customheight`. The [mangapress CLI](../../cli/mangapress/) page shows what each does.

| Format | When to choose it                                                                                                  |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| CBZ    | The format Mangabound starts with. Processed images in a ZIP archive. KOReader's own guide recommends it for manga |
| EPUB   | A book with chapter navigation and an author; the single-series-book mode requires it                              |
| PDF    | Fixed pages for a reader or tablet that supports PDF                                                               |

What works depends on the reading app as well as the device. A Kindle's own reader does not open an EPUB directly: send the file with a service that converts it, such as Amazon's Send to Kindle, or read it in an app you installed, such as KOReader. KOReader treats an EPUB differently from a CBZ or PDF. It lays the EPUB out like text, with its own margins and status bar around each page, so a page can come out smaller than the screen. See [reader settings](../../koreader/recommended-settings/).

## Advanced conversion options

**Advanced conversion options** has the settings of mangapress, grouped by purpose: **Reading**, **Double-page spreads**, **Size and borders**, **Cropping**, **Two-page view**, **Color and tone**, **Page images**, **Cover** and **Book metadata**. Start with the defaults of your device, change one setting at a time and check the book you get. If artwork is cut off, reduce cropping or turn it off.

Every group has its own page in the [overview](../advanced-options/). There, the same page is converted with and without each option, and every option is listed with the command-line flag that sets it.

Some options only matter after another. For example, pages kept in color stay JPEG even when **Page format** is PNG, until **Color pages as PNG too** is on. The controls below show this, and the [Page images](../advanced-options/page-images/) page measures what each choice costs.

<TutorialImage

  caption="In this example, color pages stay JPEG. Color pages as PNG too saves them as PNG instead."

Two options sit beside the device. **Use the images as they are** packages the pages untouched ([Page images](../advanced-options/page-images/#use-the-images-as-they-are)). **Name the book .epub** keeps a Kobo's EPUB from being named `.kepub.epub` ([Book metadata](../advanced-options/book-metadata/#name-the-book-epub)).

An option that another choice makes useless stays visible, dimmed, and says why. For example, **Webtoon strips** locks the options a webtoon never uses ([Reading](../advanced-options/reading/#what-else-changes-with-it) lists them). What you had chosen comes back when you return to pages.

You can reset a changed field on its own, or all of them with **Reset to defaults**. Your settings are kept between launches.

## One book for the series

Turn on **Create one book for the series** in the queue to combine its chapters and volumes into one book. In the table of contents, the chapters sit under their volumes.

This mode uses EPUB and keeps both process steps on. Turn it off to change the format or the steps. A large combined book can take longer to open on an e-reader.
